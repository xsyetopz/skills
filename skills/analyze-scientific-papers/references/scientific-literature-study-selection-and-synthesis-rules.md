# Study selection and synthesis rules for Scientific Literature

Use this guide after inspecting the request and target system for paper
synthesis. It selects an evidence path; it does not grant permission for an
external write or another consequential operation.

| Condition | Action | Do not substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A narrow factual question about one named paper | Open that paper and its correction/supplement first; extract only the requested evidence. | A broad literature search that dilutes the requested source. |
| A broad effectiveness or association question | Define explicit inclusion criteria and search multiple relevant indexes. | Selecting the first or most-cited paper. |
| Heterogeneous designs or outcomes | Stratify the synthesis or explain why pooling is invalid. | A single combined effect that hides incompatible measures. |
| Only an abstract or metadata record is accessible | Limit claims to what that record states and mark missing methods/results. | Invented full-text details or a nearby article. |
| A recommendation is requested | Separate empirical findings, values/tradeoffs, authoritative guidance, and your inference. | Treating a statistically significant result as a universal recommendation. |

## Unresolved decisions

A material scope, compatibility, interface, deployment, or policy choice for the
paper synthesis remains user-owned when repository evidence does not settle it.
Present concrete alternatives and consequences. Resolve routine internal details
that do not change an external contract.

## Avoiding false precision

Use thresholds, limits, versions, and acceptance criteria that govern the paper
synthesis. If none exists, report measurements or uncertainty. Do not invent a
timeout, reviewer count, confidence score, target, or error budget and then
treat it as a requirement.
